WHY THIS EXISTS
---------------
§5.2 removes NetPlumber from the wl_berkeley series with: "NetPlumber is out of
this series -- rule load scales at size^2.2-2.6 and it already deadlines at
459k rules."

The conclusion is sound; the sentence omits the limit. "Already deadlines at
459k rules" was measured on 2026-09-29 under a **3,600 s** deadline declared by
that series. The V5 reportable class is **86,400 s** -- 24 times larger. So the
claim as written reads as a statement about the engine and is in fact a
statement about a one-hour window, and NetPlumber has never been given the
reportable limit on this workload.

That matters because the k=30 cell was MARGINAL rather than hopeless. At the
cut it had completed 2,092.6 s of switch_command tasks with more in flight, and
the load exponent fitted to the two steps below it predicts a total near
2,750 s. A cell that was perhaps three quarters done when a one-hour clock
stopped it is not evidence about a 24-hour one.

WHAT IS RUN
-----------
Two cells, serially, limit_class=v5 (86,400 s wall / 32,768 MB RSS), the
machine idle, inputs generated per size in their OWN process and then reused --
§5.2's rule, because generation peaked at 13.5 GB at k=1 and measuring it
inside the run measures the harness.

  1. wl_berkeley k=30  (459,202 rules)   -- the cell the 1 h limit cut
  2. wl_berkeley k=10  (1,351,800 rules) -- the real boundary

THE GATE, DECLARED NOW SO THE BUDGET DOES NOT DECIDE IT LATER: cell 2 runs ONLY
if cell 1 produces a valid verdict. This is the 2026-09-29 series' own rule
("an engine leaves the series at its first run that is not a valid verdict and
is never retried"). If k=30 does not finish in 24 h, k=10 cannot, and running
it would manufacture a second 24 h did-not-finish that adds nothing to the
first. A skipped cell 2 is recorded as skipped, NOT as a did-not-finish (§8.1).

COST: ~12 h if both complete as predicted; 48 h plus generation if both trip
their declared limit. Either is a result.

PREDICTIONS (scored afterwards, never edited)
---------------------------------------------
N1  k=30 COMPLETES within 24 h: 0 of 520, verdict_valid.
    Basis: at the 1 h cut it had completed 2,092.6 s of load with tasks still
    running, and the 2.57 exponent from k=300->k=100 projects ~2,750 s of load
    in total -- call it 70-80 min of wall including generation. That is 5% of
    the declared limit. CONFIDENCE HIGH.
    FALSIFIED BY: any limit trip, or a verdict other than 0 of 520.

N2  k=10 COMPLETES within 24 h: 0 of 520, verdict_valid.
    Basis: 2.94x the rules of k=30 at exponent 2.57 is 13.6x the load, i.e.
    ~10.4 h, against a 24 h limit.
    CONFIDENCE LOW, and this is the cell worth being wrong about. The exponent
    is fitted to TWO steps at the bottom of the series, both under 150k rules;
    it has never been checked above 459k, and the one measurement above 147k is
    a lower bound rather than a value. An exponent of 2.9 instead of 2.57 puts
    k=10 at 17 h, and 3.2 puts it past the limit.
    FALSIFIED BY: a deadline trip -- which would be a REPORTABLE did-not-finish
    at 1.35M rules under the declared class, and the honest form of the §5.2
    claim.

N3  Peak RSS stays under 16 GB at k=10, so neither cell trips the 32 GB limit.
    Basis: net_plumber's own RSS is near-linear in rules -- 121/237/567/1,313 MB
    at 26k/58k/147k/459k, about 2.9 KB per rule -- giving ~3.9 GB at 1.35M, plus
    the generator process, which held a flat 3.58 GB across all four sizes.
    FALSIFIED BY: a memory trip, or a peak above 16 GB.

N4  Compliance stays under 1 s at BOTH sizes, so the cost remains ~100% rule
    loading. Basis: 0.0086 / 0.0177 / 0.0502 s, near-linear, giving ~0.5 s at
    1.35M rules.
    FALSIFIED BY: compliance above 5% of wall -- which would mean the cost
    profile changes with size and the "load-dominated" characterisation of
    NetPlumber on this workload is size-local.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SETTLE
-----------------------
One sample per cell, so no variance claim (the 2026-09-29 series says the same
of itself). And whatever the outcome, NetPlumber does not reach k=3 or k=1:
the same exponent puts k=3 at roughly nine days. The question here is only
WHERE the engine stops under the reportable limit, not whether it reaches full
size. It does not.

HOW §5.2 CHANGES EITHER WAY
---------------------------
  N1+N2 hold  -> "deadlines at 459k rules" is wrong at the reportable limit and
                 becomes "completes 1.35M rules in ~10 h; does not reach 4.5M".
  N1 holds,
  N2 falsified -> "completes 459k rules in ~1.2 h; does not finish 1.35M in
                 24 h". The boundary is then MEASURED rather than extrapolated.
  N1 falsified -> the original sentence stands at the reportable limit too, and
                 is restated with the limit named.

ADDENDUM 2026-10-07 -- AN A/B TO ATTRIBUTE THE RESULT, DECLARED BEFORE IT RUNS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both cells completed, and far faster than N1/N2 predicted. k=30 took 210.0 s
against a 2026-09-29 run that did not finish in 3,600 s -- better than 10x.

A 10x needs a cause, and TWO changed between the runs:
  (a) the engine. 2026-10-07 runs net_plumber at 4bdbe622, which retired the
      probe-condition technique behind -DLEGACY_CHECKS. `update_check` ran on
      EVERY flow event at a probe node, and wl_berkeley has 23 probes, 520
      checks and 459k rules, so the removed work scales with exactly the thing
      that got cheaper.
  (b) the machine. 64 GB / 16 cores here; the 2026-09-29 series recorded
      `machine: null` and predates the move.

Attributing a 10x to (a) because it is the author's own change, without
separating it from (b), is the error this campaign has already made twice --
see §7.1's two predictions scored against claims the project had withdrawn.

SO: the same cell, on the same machine, with the same inputs, against a
net_plumber built WITH -DLEGACY_CHECKS. One variable.

  limit_class=dev -- NOT reportable. This is an attribution experiment, not a
  cell of the comparison, and `dev` exists so it cannot be quoted as one.
  Deadline 3,600 s, deliberately the SAME limit the 2026-09-29 series declared,
  so the LEGACY_CHECKS arm either reproduces that did-not-finish or does not.

PREDICTION
  A1  The LEGACY_CHECKS arm does NOT finish k=30 in 3,600 s, reproducing
