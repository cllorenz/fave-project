k=10 AT -Xmx16g -- PREDICTION, declared 2026-10-02 BEFORE the run.

WHY. The 2026-10-02 series measured k=10 at 8g and k=3 and k=2 at 16g, so the
live-set exponent (0.555) spans a heap change. `max_heap_after_gc_mb` is the
smallest occupancy a GC happened to leave behind, not the live set itself, and
G1 collects less eagerly when it has room -- so the 16g points may be inflated
relative to the 8g one, and the exponent with them. Rerunning k=10 at 16g puts
the whole series on one heap and removes the confound either way.

THE PREDICTION, and it is a real one -- I expect the number to MOVE.
  live heap after GC   HIGHER than 8g's 3,519 MB; most likely 4,000-4,500 MB,
                       plausibly anywhere in 3,500-5,000.
  Reason: at 8g, a ~3.5 GB live set leaves G1 under enough pressure to run full
  collections (38 of them in that run), and a full collection compacts down to
  something near the live set. At 16g that pressure is largely gone, so the
  maximum post-GC occupancy may be sampled only after young collections, which
  leave more floating garbage behind.
  If it comes back at ~3,519 MB instead, `max_heap_after_gc_mb` is insensitive
  to -Xmx at this size, the caveat is wrong, and the 0.555 exponent stands as
  measured.

  CONSEQUENCE IF IT IS HIGHER. The k=10 -> k=3 exponent falls, and with it the
  three-point fit and the k=1 live-set estimate. At 4,200 MB the first interval
  exponent would be 0.40 (from 0.548) and k=1 would land near 11.6 GB rather
  than 12.6 GB. -Xmx 20g at k=1 survives either way; this is about the honesty
  of the exponent, not about the machine.

                   regenerated (bench/wl_berkeley/ now holds k=2, so
                   --reuse-inputs would refuse), and if generation is
                   deterministic the twelve hashes must match. If they do not,
                   the whole series is comparing different workloads.
  rule load        ~70 s     (GC pauses were only 1.25 s at 8g, so load should
                             be near heap-independent)
  compliance       ~39 s
  verdict          0 of 520, verdict_valid true
  aggregator peak  HIGHER than 8,096 MB, since committed heap may grow past
                   the 6,384 MB it reached at 8g
