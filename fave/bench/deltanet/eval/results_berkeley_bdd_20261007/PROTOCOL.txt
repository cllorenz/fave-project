THIS IS NOT A REPEAT. TWO THINGS DIFFER, AND BOTH ARE STATED BEFORE THE RUN.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE HEAP WAS NEVER SET. Those cells record `jvm_xmx: null`, so they ran on
   the JVM DEFAULT -- a quarter of RAM. §5.2's own gotcha list says why that
   matters: "Set -Xmx explicitly; the JVM default is a quarter of RAM and its
   OOM reads like an engine limit." At k=10 the aggregator reached 7,873 MB,
   which on a box whose default heap was ~8 GB means the run was at or near its
   ceiling. The "memory pressure is plausible" in the lost cell's record is
   therefore more than plausible: k=3 is 3.3x k=10 against a heap that k=10 had
   already filled.
2. THE MACHINE IS DIFFERENT AND WAS NEVER STAMPED. Those cells record
   `machine: null` -- the absence item 31 exists to fix. They were taken before
   the move to this 64 GB box.

So this run answers a question the 2026-09-29 series could not pose, and its
numbers are NOT directly comparable with that table's. They ARE comparable with
phase B's NDD rows, which is the point: same box, same harness, explicit heap.

WHAT IS RUN
-----------
One cell, through `bench/deltanet/eval/engine_run.py` -- the SAME entry point
phase B's NDD cells used, so the two engines differ in the engine and nothing
else:

  generate k=3 in its own process (test/gen_deltanet_inputs.sh), then
  engine_run.py wl_berkeley --backend apkeep --engine bdd --keep-every 3
      --reuse-inputs --jvm-xmx 24g --memory-floor 2000 --deadline 86400

  -Xmx24g, chosen and declared: k=10 used ~7.9 GB at 1.35M rules, k=3 is 3.31x
  that, so ~26 GB is the linear projection and 24g is deliberately BELOW it --
  if the live set really is linear this will not fit, and that is the answer.
  Phase B's NDD at k=1 (13.4M rules) needed 13.35 GB live at -Xmx20g, so if BDD
  needs more than 24g at a THIRD of the rules, that gap is the finding.

  NO 32 GB RSS CAP. Phase B's NDD cells ran this way too (drill path,
  --memory-floor 2000 only), and capping one engine and not the other would
  make the comparison meaningless. An OOM against the 64 GB box is a faithful
  result (§8.2) and is NOT retried smaller.
